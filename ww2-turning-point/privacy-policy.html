Privacy Policy

WW2: Turning Point (iOS) · Effective 23 October 2026 · Applies to version 0.1.0 and later until this page says otherwise

The short version

WW2: Turning Point does not collect any personal data. The game has no accounts and no sign-in. It contains no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. It does not send your data to us or to anyone else.

What stays on your device

To let you resume a war, the game saves the following on your iPhone, inside the app's own storage:

  • your campaign save;
  • story progress;
  • settings such as difficulty, speed, map style, label size and haptics.

This data never leaves your device through the game, and we cannot see it. Deleting the app deletes it. Your device's own backups (for example iCloud Backup, if you have it switched on) may include app data. Those backups are governed by Apple's terms and your settings, not by us.

Network use

The game runs offline. All maps, story text and images are bundled with the app, and the game makes no network requests of its own.

Some screens contain links, such as the map-data credit and source notes on story cards. A link opens only when you tap it, and it opens outside the game. From then on, the website you visit has its own privacy practices.

Share card and Photos

When a war ends, you can create a share card image. The image is made on your device. It goes somewhere only if you choose a destination in the iOS share sheet, and we never receive it. If you choose "Save Image", iOS asks for permission to add the image to your Photos library. The game only adds that one image and does not read your library.

Children

The game collects no data from anyone, including children.

Third parties

The game includes no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking software. Apple may collect data about downloads and crashes under its own privacy policy and your device settings. If you choose to share diagnostics with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple may pass crash reports to us. They do not identify you.

Changes

If a future version changes any of this, for example by adding online features, this page will be updated before that version is released. The effective date above will change too.
